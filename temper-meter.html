How it decides

Every tenth of a second it measures three things: how loud you are, the pitch of your voice, and how sharp or harsh it sounds (where the energy sits in the frequency spectrum). Angry speech is usually louder, higher, and sharper than the same person's calm speech.

Each measure is compared with your calm baseline and combined into a 0–100 score, smoothed over about a second. Under 45 is calm, 45–65 is tense, above 65 is angry.

Calibrate once for much better results. Without it, the meter compares you with a generic voice. It can't tell anger from excitement, shouting across a room, or a loud laugh, and it ignores the words you say.
